Transaction: Cash-Out Refinance (Conventional)
Current mortgage: Wells Fargo, $271K balance @ 3.75%, payment $1,408/mo
New loan: $416,000 @ 6.5%, 30-yr fixed (80% LTV on $520K appraised)

Michael and Sarah bought their home in March 2021 for $380,000 with a $304,000 mortgage
at 3.75%. The property has appreciated significantly - it just appraised for $520,000.
They've built up about $249K in equity and want to do a cash-out refinance.

The main reason is debt consolidation. Over the last couple years they accumulated about
$78,000 in credit card debt - a mix of medical expenses from Sarah's surgery last year,
some emergency home repairs (AC replacement), and just general cost of living increases.
The cards are averaging about 22% APR, so they're paying $2,340 a month just in minimum
credit card payments. It's killing them.

They're also looking to do a kitchen and bathroom remodel, estimated at about $41,000.

So the plan: new loan of $416,000 at 6.5%. That pays off the $271K existing mortgage,
the $78K in credit cards, and about $16K in closing costs. They walk away with about
$51,000 in cash - $41K for the remodel and $10K to keep as reserves.

The numbers work. Combined income is $17,250 a month - Michael makes $135K at Intel as
a software engineer and Sarah makes $72K as a marketing director. After the consolidation,
their new PITIA is $3,274 and their remaining debt is just an auto loan at $520 and
student loan at $300. Back-end DTI comes to about 24%, which is very clean.

But here's the thing - if you don't exclude the credit card debt from DTI because it's
being paid off at closing, the DTI jumps to 37.3% and they fail conventional guidelines.
The key is recognizing that those three credit cards ($2,340/mo total) are being retired
as part of this transaction.

Credit is decent - Michael at 705, Sarah at 698. The scores are a bit lower than you'd
expect for their income because of the high utilization on those credit cards. Once the
consolidation goes through, utilization drops and their scores should jump 30-50 points.

Payment history on the existing mortgage is perfect - zero late payments in the last 24
months. They've owned the home for over 5 years, well past the seasoning requirement.

This is a solid cash-out refi. The rate is going up from 3.75% to 6.5% which is a big
jump, but the debt consolidation makes it worth it - they're saving $1,119 a month net
after accounting for the higher mortgage payment. Next steps: get the title commitment,
verify the payoff statement, and submit to DU.
