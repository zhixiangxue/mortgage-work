## My read

Good Non-QM 1099 file with a clear analytical crux. Robert has 4 years of growing 1099
income ($132K -> $145K, +9.8% YoY) from blue-chip tech clients through reputable staffing
agencies. Credit is clean at 738. The property is a well-maintained remodeled SFR in
a good San Diego neighborhood.

The make-or-break issue is the income calculation method. The CPA P&L with actual expenses
($10,125/mo) produces a 43.1% back-end DTI that passes the Non-QM 50% cap. The standard
25% expense factor ($8,656/mo) produces 50.4% that fails by 0.4 percentage points.

The CPA-prepared P&L is the recommended approach because:
1. Actual expenses ($17K/yr) are much lower than the 25% factor ($34,625/yr)
2. The expense breakdown is reasonable for an IT contractor (home office, equipment)
3. The CPA is licensed and has attested to the accuracy
